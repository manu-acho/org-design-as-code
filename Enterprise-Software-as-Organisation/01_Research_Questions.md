# Research Questions

## Primary question

**How are organisational coordination and governance mechanisms encoded within enterprise software reference architectures?**

The question addresses a gap between studies of organisational implementation and the prior design of packaged capabilities. Thompson, Galbraith, and Mintzberg provide the principal theoretical vocabulary. Evidence must connect official SAP artefacts to identifiable primitives and then to mechanisms through an auditable inference. The unit of analysis is an encoded organisational arrangement: a bounded configuration of software constructs governing differentiated activities. Analysis combines within-process mechanism maps with cross-process comparison. The expected contribution is an empirically disciplined account of enterprise software as codified organisation design. The principal limitation is that documentary evidence establishes neither implementation nor enactment. Later papers will test movement between those levels.

## RQ1: Interdependence and coordination

**How do SAP S/4HANA reference processes encode different forms of organisational interdependence and coordination?**

The motivation is to move beyond generic claims that ERP “integrates” work. Thompson's pooled, sequential, and reciprocal interdependence and Mintzberg's coordination mechanisms specify what integration may consist of. Required evidence includes ordered process steps, shared resources, iterative loops, role handoffs, prerequisites, status transitions, and exception paths in P2P, O2C, and R2R. The primary unit is a dependency relation between differentiated activities; the meso unit is the process configuration. Dependency graphs will be paired with mechanism codes. Contribution depends on showing systematic correspondences or theoretically meaningful departures, not merely relabelling arrows. Documentation may omit informal or reciprocal work. A future implementation study can compare encoded and enacted dependencies.

## RQ2: Roles, rights, visibility, and control

**How does SAP's enterprise architecture distribute organisational roles, decision rights, information access, and control?**

This question links organisation and decision theory with SAP's role, authorisation, organisational restriction, approval, and monitoring constructs. Evidence must distinguish template roles from configured roles and app availability from data access. Units include a right–actor–object–scope relation and configurations such as initiator/approver/recorder separation. Matrices will map actions and objects to roles, restrictions, decisions, and accountability traces. The expected contribution is a mechanism-level account of encoded governance. Technical permission does not prove exercised authority, and segregation of duties must not be inferred without explicit evidence. Later papers can compare reference allocations with customer designs.

## RQ3: Standardisation and discretion

**What forms of standardisation, organisational discretion, exception handling, and escalation are embedded within SAP reference processes?**

The question rejects a binary opposition between standard and flexible. Mintzberg's forms of standardisation are combined with empirically differentiated discretion: mandatory, configurable, extensible, exceptional, and—only if documented—overridable. Evidence includes required fields, predefined workflow scenarios, configuration activities, condition rules, released extension points, blocks, deadlines, substitutions, and exception consequences. The unit is a constrained choice or rule in relation to an organisational task. Analysis locates who can exercise discretion, over what, when, and with what trace. Contribution would recast configuration as bounded organisational discretion. Public documentation may underdescribe limits and licence dependencies. A later comparative study can examine customer configuration decisions.

## RQ4: Relation to organisation theory

**How do these arrangements correspond to, combine, or depart from classical theories of organisation design and coordination?**

This question prevents theory from serving only as a coding vocabulary. Evidence consists of supported mechanism patterns, counter-examples, overlaps, and constructs not adequately explained by the three primary lenses. The unit is the theoretically interpreted pattern, supported by multiple observations. Pattern matching will ask where categories converge, where one software construct realises several mechanisms, and where architectural mechanisms—such as machine-enforced status gating—require conceptual refinement. Contribution may be extension, boundary specification, or rejection; novelty is not presumed. Theory forcing is the major risk. Future work can test revised constructs across platforms.

## RQ5: Fit-to-Standard

**What organisational assumptions are embedded within SAP's Fit-to-Standard logic?**

Fit-to-Standard may make the encounter between vendor template and adopting organisation visible. The theoretical basis is organisation design under bounded choice, complemented by enterprise-systems misfit research. Paper 1 requires official method descriptions, reference-process content, configuration boundaries, and extension guidance. The unit is an articulated fit decision or vendor-prescribed comparison point—not an actual workshop interaction unless observed. Analysis will identify which organisational dimensions are made discussable and which are backgrounded. The contribution is presently conceptual and sensitising. Without workshop data the study cannot claim negotiation outcomes. Paper 2 will require observations, interviews, backlog items, and design decisions.

## Question hierarchy and stopping rules

RQ1–RQ3 produce empirical descriptions; RQ4 supplies theoretical reconstruction; RQ5 is secondary and must not displace the architectural evidence. A candidate answer is retained only when it has traceable evidence, an explicit inference, a rival interpretation, and a stated scope. Claims about organisational outcomes, employee behaviour, implementation success, or performance are outside Paper 1.
