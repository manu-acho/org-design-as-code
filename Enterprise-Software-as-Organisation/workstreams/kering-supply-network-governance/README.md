# Kering Upstream Supply-Network Governance

## Purpose

This workstream examines how coordination, authority, traceability, control, and accountability are represented across the digital, contractual, and procedural interfaces connecting a luxury group with a multi-tier artisan production ecosystem.

Kering is the proposed organisational case. Its inclusion is provisional until the feasibility audit establishes an adequate public documentary corpus, a defensible unit of analysis, and a viable route to any non-public evidence required by later research stages.

## Position in the wider programme

This is a separate empirical workstream within the Enterprise Software as Organisation research programme. It extends the analytical lens from intra-organisational enterprise reference architecture to inter-organisational supply-network governance.

It does not create a tenth foundational literature stream. It reuses the authoritative [nine-stream literature map](../../03_Literature_Map.md) and adds only the context-specific scholarship needed to understand firm boundaries, value-chain governance, due diligence, inter-organisational information systems, and artisan production ecosystems.

## Provisional central question

> How are coordination, authority, traceability, control, and accountability encoded across the digital, contractual, and procedural interfaces connecting a luxury group with its multi-tier artisan production ecosystem?

## Supporting questions

1. How are boundaries drawn among internal production, strategic partnerships, direct suppliers, and subcontractors?
2. How are information requirements, decision rights, monitoring, and corrective action distributed across organisational tiers?
3. How does the represented governance architecture attempt to balance traceability and accountability with supplier autonomy, flexibility, and craft knowledge?
4. What evidence would be required to distinguish represented architecture, implemented arrangements, and enacted supplier practice?
5. How are the costs, capabilities, and administrative burdens associated with digital accountability distributed between the lead firm and smaller suppliers?

## Analytical levels

| Level | Object of study | Minimum evidence | Permitted claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| Architectural coherence | Public policies, standards, portals, reporting structures, contracts or contract templates, audit protocols, and represented processes | Versioned documentary corpus with source provenance | What the represented governance architecture requires, permits, restricts, or leaves unspecified |
| Implementation fit | Systems, workflows, roles, local procedures, integrations, and organisational arrangements actually introduced | Interviews and implementation evidence from relevant organisational actors | How the represented architecture was translated into an implemented arrangement |
| Enacted fit | Actual supplier, workshop, worker, auditor, and intermediary practices | Field evidence with appropriate ethics and triangulation | How actors use, interpret, adapt, contest, or work around the implemented arrangement |

Public documents alone support only the first level. They cannot establish implementation effectiveness, supplier behaviour, working conditions, living-wage outcomes, audit effects, or organisational performance.

## Non-claims

Until supported by appropriate evidence, this workstream must not claim that:

- public policies describe actual supplier practice;
- Kering has achieved full multi-tier traceability;
- unauthorised subcontracting occurs within a particular Kering supply chain;
- audits improve labour, environmental, or commercial outcomes;
- digital onboarding benefits or burdens smaller suppliers in a particular way;
- any particular enterprise system, PLM system, blockchain, or digital product passport architecture is in operational use;
- the case represents the luxury sector as a whole.

## Immediate scope

The workstream is currently limited to research design and feasibility. Full contextual literature collection and empirical analysis should begin only after the shared literature architecture and the SAP architectural-coherence pilot are sufficiently stable to prevent duplication and method drift.
