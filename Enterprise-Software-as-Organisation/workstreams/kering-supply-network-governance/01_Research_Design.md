# Research Design

## Design logic

The proposed study is an embedded organisational case of inter-organisational supply-network governance. Its initial phase is a documentary reconstruction of represented governance architecture. Later phases may examine implementation fit and enacted fit only if access and ethics requirements are satisfied.

The study is analytically related to the SAP reference-architecture research but is not part of the SAP corpus. It has a different unit of analysis, evidence base, legal context, access problem, and claim boundary.

## Unit of analysis

The provisional unit of analysis is:

> A governance interface through which an identified organisational actor communicates information, assigns or exercises a right, applies a standard, coordinates a dependency, records a traceability event, authorises an exception, or initiates monitoring, correction, escalation, suspension, or termination across an organisational boundary.

Candidate interfaces include supplier onboarding, qualification, vendor rating, subcontractor authorisation, order and capacity coordination, audit, corrective action, certification, traceability reporting, access control, and escalation.

This definition must be tested during the feasibility audit. The study must not alternate silently among Kering Group, individual Houses, product categories, industrial districts, direct suppliers, subcontractors, and workers as if they were one unit.

## Case boundaries to freeze

Before corpus collection, record explicit decisions for:

- organisational boundary: Group, selected Houses, or both;
- geographic boundary: Italy, France, or a justified comparison;
- production boundary: one product category or a controlled set;
- supplier-tier boundary: direct suppliers, authorised subcontractors, and any deeper tiers supported by evidence;
- process boundary: sourcing, onboarding, subcontracting, production coordination, audit, remediation, and related payment or suspension decisions;
- time boundary: a dated reporting and policy period;
- regulatory boundary: the exact legal instruments and versions applicable during that period;
- comparison boundary: no comparison, within-case comparison, or an explicitly designed external contrast.

## Study stages

### Stage 0: Feasibility and source audit

Establish whether the official documentary corpus can support a bounded reconstruction. Verify the legal baseline, identify source versions, test the proposed unit of analysis, and record critical evidence gaps. No empirical findings are produced at this stage.

### Stage 1: Architectural coherence

Analyse how the documentary architecture represents actors, dependencies, information, rights, standards, controls, exceptions, and consequences. Compare artefacts for internal consistency and identify omissions, tensions, and rival interpretations.

### Stage 2: Implementation fit

If access is obtained, examine how relevant systems, contracts, roles, and workflows were configured and integrated. Evidence must come from actors with direct implementation knowledge and from implementation records where available.

### Stage 3: Enacted fit

If ethically and practically feasible, examine how suppliers, workshops, workers, auditors, and intermediaries experience and enact the arrangements. This stage requires an approved fieldwork protocol and must not be inferred from Stages 0 to 2.

## Analytical framework

The workstream reuses the shared programme concepts while preserving their differences:

- organisation design identifies differentiated activities and integration arrangements;
- coordination theory connects dependencies to represented mechanisms;
- decision theory distinguishes premises, programmes, permissions, authority, and judgement;
- enterprise-systems research distinguishes package or platform capabilities from implementation choices;
- routines research separates procedural representations from recurrent performance;
- materiality and practice research limits deterministic inference;
- infrastructure research examines standards, identifiers, classifications, installed bases, and interconnection;
- governance and control distinguish monitoring, intervention, answerability, and consequences;
- process architecture identifies events, actors, objects, states, boundaries, hand-offs, variation, and exceptions.

## Candidate contribution

The study may evaluate the usefulness of the provisional concept of an **inter-organisational governance primitive**: a recurring digital, contractual, or procedural element that performs an identifiable governance operation across an organisational boundary.

Candidate examples include supplier identity, qualification status, authorised subcontractor, traceability event, audit request, certification, corrective action, access permission, approval, exception, escalation, suspension, and termination.

These are sensitising concepts, not findings. A primitive designation requires documentary evidence of both the represented operation and the organisational relation it structures.

## Comparative value

The workstream can later support a disciplined comparison between intra-organisational software architecture and inter-organisational governance architecture. Such comparison must occur at the level of mechanisms and relations, not by equating SAP artefacts with Kering policies or supplier practices.
