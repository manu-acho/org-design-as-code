# Ethics and Access Protocol

## Purpose

Later stages of this workstream may involve commercially sensitive information and participants whose livelihoods depend on lead firms, direct suppliers, or intermediaries. Access must never be treated as permission to expose identifiable organisations or individuals.

## Stage gates

### Public-document stage

No participant data are collected. The study must still distinguish allegation, corporate disclosure, legal requirement, researcher inference, and independently verified observation.

### Organisational-interview stage

Before interviewing Kering, House, supplier, auditor, technology-provider, or intermediary personnel, establish:

- research-ethics approval or documented determination of the applicable process;
- informed-consent materials;
- recording and transcription rules;
- data-retention and access controls;
- organisational and individual anonymisation options;
- treatment of commercially confidential material;
- procedures for withdrawal and quotation approval where applicable.

### Supplier and worker fieldwork stage

Before involving workshops, subcontractors, or workers, add controls for:

- power asymmetry and possible coercion;
- recruitment that does not pass solely through management;
- safe and private participation;
- language and literacy accessibility;
- protection against commercial or employment retaliation;
- separation of research from audit or compliance activity;
- handling allegations of harm or unlawful conduct;
- cross-border data transfer and storage;
- aggregation or masking of locations, product categories, and supply-chain relationships.

## Access matrix

| Actor group | Potential evidence | Main risk | Minimum safeguard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Group and House personnel | Governance intent, role allocation, system and implementation choices | Corporate attribution and confidentiality | Role-based anonymisation and documented data-use agreement |
| Direct suppliers | Onboarding, coordination, audit, data, and commercial requirements | Commercial dependency | Recruitment and reporting that protect supplier identity |
| Subcontractors and workshops | Actual subcontracting, digital burden, exceptions, and work practices | Retaliation and re-identification | Independent recruitment where feasible and strong identity masking |
| Workers and representatives | Working conditions, voice, and experienced consequences | Employment risk and vulnerability | Private consent process, minimal data collection, and protected reporting |
| Auditors and intermediaries | Monitoring, remediation, and escalation practices | Client confidentiality and role conflict | Clear boundary between research evidence and audit records |

## Claim discipline

Participant accounts establish what a participant reports or understands. They do not independently establish the complete supply-chain condition. Sensitive claims require appropriate corroboration, careful qualification, and an explicit decision about whether publication could cause harm.

## Stop conditions

Pause fieldwork if:

- participation cannot be made meaningfully voluntary;
- anonymisation cannot adequately protect a vulnerable participant;
- a gatekeeper demands identifiable participant responses;
- the research could be mistaken for a corporate audit or enforcement process;
- data security is inadequate for the material being requested;
- the proposed claim cannot be published without exposing a participant or supplier to disproportionate risk.
