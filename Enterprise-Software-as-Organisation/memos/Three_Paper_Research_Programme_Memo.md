# Planning memo: Three papers from a shared research programme

**Status:** Agreed direction for planning. Paper 1 is a working manuscript. The Organisation Design spin-off is a feasibility study, not an approved manuscript. The Digital Transformation study remains dependent on access to implementation evidence. This memo does not change the [existing paper pipeline](../12_Paper_Pipeline.md) or assign a new paper number.

## Decision

The three original abstract framings identified different audiences, but changing the audience alone would not justify three papers. The opportunity is to ask three different questions about related material: **what the reference architecture encodes, how its alternative routes organise work differently, and what organisations do with those alternatives during implementation.**

The literature review, bibliography, release-controlled corpus and source-locator system are shared research assets. Each manuscript should reuse them selectively, with a distinct question, analytical unit and contribution. Reusing a case to orient the reader is different from publishing the same finding twice.

| Framing | Main question | Unit of analysis | Evidence and readiness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Information Systems | How does enterprise software encode coordination and governance? | A documented operation in relation to the work it connects, assigns or constrains | Current Swiss SAP reference corpus. Working manuscript exists. |
| Organisation Design | When alternative routes reach the same type of result, how do they redistribute work, information and decisions? | A matched contrast between documented routes, including the result held comparable and the arrangement that changes | Existing cases are the starting point. A fresh comparative analysis must demonstrate a distinct result before drafting a standalone paper. |
| Digital Transformation | How do organisations choose, adapt or reject reference arrangements during Fit-to-Standard? | An implementation design decision traced from the reference process through workshop, configuration and testing | The reference corpus is the baseline. Customer-specific implementation evidence is needed. |

## Finish Paper 1 first

The [Information Systems manuscript](../paper/README.md) remains the foundation. Its contribution is to make the enterprise software artefact inspectable, using organisation design to interpret what documented operations do. The next editorial pass should test the argument, pacing and evidence boundaries. It should not remove relevant findings merely to reserve them for another paper. Paper 1 can describe represented arrangements, but it cannot establish implementation fit, enacted work or realised outcomes.

## Test the Organisation Design spin-off before drafting

**Working proposition:** A reference design can standardise the type of result without standardising the arrangement of work that produces it. This is a proposition to test, not a finding about operational performance or functional equivalence.

Start with two contrasts already identified in Paper 1. In J58, direct journal posting and verification before posting reach the same type of accounting state, but the verification route introduces an unposted submission, a processor decision and a return path. In J45, PO-referenced and no-PO invoice routes can both lead to posting, but differ in their reliance on upstream records and in what the accountant supplies. These examples establish the question. Because both appear in Paper 1, they cannot on their own establish the new paper's contribution.

1. Build a small comparison matrix from the release-controlled cases. For each pair record the comparable result, differences in required information, task ownership, decision points, checks and exception routes, and the exact evidence locators.
2. State why each comparison is valid. A shared status label does not prove equivalent accounting effects, risk, effort or quality. Record differences in object, scope or purpose that could explain an apparent contrast.
3. Seek disconfirming comparisons. Look for a route that changes the result, an apparent variant that changes only the interface, and cases where the documentation does not show enough to compare the division of work. Record them rather than forcing a pattern.
4. Compare the resulting argument with Paper 1. Proceed only if it explains **what changes and what stays fixed across alternative designs**, with evidence and counterexamples beyond what a reader can already conclude from Paper 1. If the current corpus cannot sustain that result, extend the documentary evidence or retain the analysis as a memo rather than writing a second article.

The theoretical emphasis would shift from identifying software operations to explaining alternative arrangements of differentiated work. Thompson, Mintzberg and Galbraith remain relevant, but the paper would use them to compare design choices, not to reclassify the same list of features.

## Prepare the Digital Transformation study in parallel

The existing pipeline already includes *Fit-to-Standard as Organisational Design Negotiation*. Use the Swiss reference material to identify what was offered at the start of a design decision, not as a substitute for evidence of the decision itself.

For a small number of consequential decisions, try to trace **reference route → workshop discussion and decision → configuration or extension → test evidence**. An approval dispute is a useful example: an organisation may agree that a control is needed while disagreeing about who should exercise it or what should happen when an exception occurs. Relevant evidence could include authorised workshop observation, decision records, backlog items, configuration and test records, and interviews that explain why alternatives were accepted or rejected.

Access planning can proceed while Paper 1 is being edited and the Organisation Design comparison is being tested. If access only covers part of the chain, narrow the question to that part. Do not infer enactment from workshop agreement or a successful test. Claims about work after go-live require separate evidence of actual practice.

## Next decisions

- Finish the Paper 1 editorial pass and keep its contribution intact.
- Prepare the matched-route comparison and decide whether it supports an independent Organisation Design paper.
- In parallel, scope the implementation evidence and access needed for the existing Fit-to-Standard study.

Use the same test at each step: **what could a reader conclude from this paper that they could not already conclude from Paper 1?**
