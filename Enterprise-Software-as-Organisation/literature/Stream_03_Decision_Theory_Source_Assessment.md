# Stream Three: Decision Theory Source Assessment

## Audit method

The LeapSpace report was treated as a candidate-source map. Publication identity and substantive access were checked against publisher, journal, professional-association, or institutional-repository records. Access labels distinguish **full text read**, **abstract read**, **metadata verified**, and **unresolved**. A full-text route located but not substantively inspected is not labelled as read.

## Evidential corrections

1. Software rules should not be described as human judgement.
2. A technical permission does not establish authority in Simon’s sense.
3. A workflow approval may allocate a decision opportunity, but it does not prove deliberation or accepted authority.
4. Automated execution may implement premises chosen elsewhere and may relocate discretion rather than eliminate it.
5. Traceability records events; accountability additionally requires answerability and consequences.
6. A programme represented in documentation is not the same as a routine enacted in practice.
7. The presence of more data does not dissolve bounded rationality or demonstrate better decisions.
8. Public-administration evidence on system-level bureaucracy supplies a sensitising mechanism, not direct evidence about SAP customers.

## Remaining verification work

- Verify the exact copies and pagination of *Administrative Behavior*, *Organizations*, *A Behavioral Theory of the Firm*, and *The Functions of the Executive* before using quotations or page citations.
- Obtain and read the full text of Puranam et al. (2015), Brunsson (1990), and Bromiley et al. (2019) before assigning detailed claims.
- Verify the version-of-record metadata for Simon (1973, 1976, 1979), Cohen et al. (1972), and Feldman and March (1981).
- Inspect the institutional full text of Bovens and Zouridis (2002) before transferring specific mechanisms beyond its publisher abstract.
- Resolve the unidentified “Decision Theory.pdf” only if it can be mapped to stable scholarly publications. Do not cite the upload label.

### SciSpace claims explicitly rejected or narrowed

- The proposition that enterprise systems relocate “the majority” of operational discretion is unsupported and too broad. The retained claim is that some discretion may be relocated, subject to documentary evidence.
- A deterministic algorithm is not automatically outside the category of decision. Calculation, classification, selection, action, and organisational attribution must be coded separately.
- Absence of transaction-time human selection does not prove that discretion was relocated specifically to configuration. Premises may originate in product design, law, policy, data governance, models, or external services.
- Permission for a subsequent step to proceed does not automatically make an operation an approval.
- Spreadsheet use would not falsify the existence of system-encoded premises; it may indicate supplementation, workaround, or distrust.
- SAP-specific examples such as tax calculation, account derivation, search helps, configuration tables, and segregation-of-duties blocks require official SAP corpus evidence. Decision-theory papers cannot verify those product details.
