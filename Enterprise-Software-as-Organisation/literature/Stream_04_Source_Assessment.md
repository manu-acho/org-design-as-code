# Stream Four: Source Assessment

## Assessment of the supplied LeapSpace report

The supplied report is not a completed literature review. It is retained as a discovery record only.

Major defects:

1. References 1, 3, 4, and 6 are the uploaded prompt rather than scholarship.
2. The foundational Davenport, Markus and Tanis, Soh et al., and Boudreau and Robey works were listed without full bibliographic assessment in the report's own table.
3. The table uses opaque identifiers such as "2-1-45" instead of citations.
4. Several unrelated implementation studies were used to create a macro, meso, and micro implementation claim.
5. The SAP-specific argument rests mainly on abstracts, practitioner books, and vendor-adjacent sources.
6. Fit-to-Standard was discussed without a sufficient peer-reviewed evidence base.
7. Claims that architecture "redistributes coordination" or that cloud ERP narrows discretion were stronger than the evidence supplied.
8. The requested learning guide, full scholarly synthesis, technical expansion, conceptual matrix, and complete access audit were not delivered.

## Assessment of the supplied SciSpace report

The SciSpace report is substantially more complete than the LeapSpace report and contains several valuable source leads. It is nevertheless not accepted as evidence in its own right.

Material corrections and limits:

1. Several strong claims about public-cloud ERP, mandatory updates, reduced discretion, and Fit-to-Standard exceed the evidence of the cited studies.
2. "Vanilla implementation" and SAP Fit-to-Standard are related but not interchangeable concepts.
3. The report presents public-cloud constraints as general facts even though some supporting studies concern other cloud products, consultant expectations, or small case samples.
4. Reference-number reuse and formatting make some claim-to-source relationships difficult to audit.
5. Several items reported as full-text access still require independent verification against the original publication before page-specific use.
6. Material on workarounds, appropriation, and technology-in-use is useful but belongs primarily in the forthcoming routines and technology-in-practice reviews.
7. Pollock and Williams (2010) concerns strategic ethnography and infrastructure research. It should not substitute for the more directly relevant Pollock, Procter, and Williams (2003) ERP-package study.
8. The report correctly weakens the novelty claim by identifying prior research that directly analyses packages and architecture as organisational artefacts.

## Sources absent from LeapSpace but required

- Robey, Ross, and Boudreau (2002) on learning and dialectics of enterprise-system implementation.
- Hong and Kim (2002) on organisational fit, adaptation, and resistance.
- Strong and Volkoff (2010) on the enterprise-system artefact and multidimensional fit.
- Shang and Seddon (2002) on benefit categories and longitudinal assessment.
- Soh and Sia on the problems of supposedly vanilla implementations.
- Post-implementation assimilation research, including Liang et al. (2007), for later extension.
- Current peer-reviewed research specifically on public-cloud ERP and Fit-to-Standard. This remains the largest evidence gap.

The SciSpace report partially addressed the first-generation cloud ERP gap through Peng and Gala (2014) and Bjelland and Haddara (2018). It did not close the narrower gap concerning contemporary SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition, Fit-to-Standard enactment, clean-core constraints, or continuous-release organisational effects.

## Verification priorities

1. Obtain the exact Markus and Tanis chapter.
2. Obtain the full Soh, Kien, and Tay-Yap article.
3. Verify the full Strong and Volkoff study before operationalising its domains.
4. Retrieve the full Volkoff, Strong, and Elmes paper.
5. Audit recent peer-reviewed public-cloud ERP research independently of SAP terminology.
6. Treat Fit-to-Standard outcome claims as unestablished until independent empirical evidence is located.
