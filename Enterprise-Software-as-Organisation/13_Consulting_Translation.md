# Consulting Translation

The research can support transformation practice by making organisational consequences discussable before configuration decisions harden. It complements, rather than replaces, SAP Activate's process and delivery disciplines.

**Fit-to-Standard versus Fit-to-Coordination.** A fit discussion normally asks whether standard functionality supports a requirement. A coordination lens asks which dependency is being managed, what information is required, who holds the decision right, how exceptions are resolved, and where discretion should remain. The output is a traceable design decision, not an argument for customisation.

**Coordination diagnosis.** Map activity dependencies and current mechanisms, then compare them with the reference process. Identify mechanism gaps, duplications, and shifts—for example, a local mutual-adjustment practice represented as rule-based workflow. The diagnosis does not assume the local practice is superior; it makes the trade-off explicit.

**Decision-right mapping.** For each material decision, document actor, object, scope, information, threshold, allowed outcomes, escalation, and trace. Compare this map with business roles, authorisations, workflows, and organisational restrictions. This bridges organisation design, security design, and process governance without collapsing them.

**Process-governance diagnosis.** Separate ownership, approval, execution, monitoring, and exception responsibility. Test whether controls have a specified standard, observer, and consequence and whether segregation requirements are evidenced rather than assumed.

**Standardisation implications.** Classify each design element as mandatory, configurable, extensible, exceptional, or outside scope. Ask what is standardised—work, output, information category, or right—and at which organisational level. This produces a more discriminating transformation architecture than a generic standard/custom ratio.

These practices complement Activate project management and business transformation expertise by connecting workshop decisions to operating-model questions. Their value depends on evidence and facilitation quality; the research should not be used to claim performance benefits before those are evaluated.
