# Assessment Strategy

## Assessment philosophy

The assessments look at how well you connect theory to evidence, show where your observations come from, and explain what the evidence can support. Memorising SAP terms will not substitute for that analysis.

## Provisional postgraduate assessment pattern

| Assessment | Weight | Primary outcomes | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Weekly analytical portfolio | 20% | 2 to 10 | Develop recognition, differentiation, and evidence discipline |
| Theory comparison paper | 20% | 1, 4, 5, 7 | Compare what different theories reveal and leave unresolved |
| Process architecture analysis | 25% | 3 to 10, 12 | Conduct a traceable analysis of one bounded process or artefact set |
| Integrative comparative project | 35% | 11, 12 | Compare processes or configurations and defend bounded conclusions |

The weighting is provisional and may vary by institution. The assessment design combines individual theoretical work with an applied analysis that brings the course together.

## Assessment briefs

### Weekly analytical portfolio

Students complete short exercises in process reconstruction, dependency identification, mechanism classification, information tracing, knowledge-boundary analysis, and decision-premise analysis. The draft portfolio brief asks you to revise selected entries so the portfolio shows how your reasoning develops.

The [draft brief](assessments/Draft_Core_Assessment_Briefs.md#1-weekly-analytical-portfolio-20) identifies which practice entries to revise and submit. Some exercises also rehearse outcomes outside this component's primary outcome range. They are not additional graded components.

### Theory comparison paper

Students analyse one organisational problem through two or three theoretical lenses. A strong paper explains what each perspective makes visible, how the perspectives relate, and which questions remain outside their reach.

See the [draft brief](assessments/Draft_Core_Assessment_Briefs.md#2-theory-comparison-paper-20) for the two-lens default and planning range.

### Process architecture analysis

Students select a controlled artefact set and produce:

- an evidence register;
- a bounded process or architecture reconstruction;
- descriptive observations;
- theoretical interpretations;
- rival explanations;
- an inference-boundary statement;
- a short reflection on missing evidence.

See the [draft brief](assessments/Draft_Core_Assessment_Briefs.md#3-process-architecture-analysis-25) for the process boundary, case requirements and planning range.

### Integrative comparative project

Students compare at least two process or architecture configurations. They must explain similarities and differences through documented dependencies and arrangements rather than assuming that one design represents universal best practice. The submission includes a written analysis and oral defence. See the [draft comparative project brief](assessments/M10_Integrative_Comparative_Project.md) for provisional deliverables and marking guidance.

## Core marking dimensions

| Dimension | Indicative weight | Excellent performance |
|---|---:|---|
| Conceptual accuracy | 20% | Uses concepts precisely and preserves material distinctions |
| Evidence quality and traceability | 20% | Every important observation is locatable and appropriately scoped |
| Analytical reasoning | 25% | Connects dependency, mechanism, information, decision, and governance coherently |
| Rival interpretations and boundaries | 15% | Tests alternatives and states what the evidence cannot establish |
| Integration and originality | 10% | Produces a defensible synthesis rather than a feature inventory |
| Communication | 10% | Presents a readable, logically sequenced, and professionally documented argument |

## Academic integrity and AI use

Institutional rules will govern permitted AI use. At minimum, students must remain responsible for every citation, factual statement, interpretation, and submitted artefact. Fabricated citations, invented SAP functionality, and unverified vendor claims are unacceptable regardless of the tool used to produce them.

If generative AI is permitted, an assessment may require an audit note recording:

- the tool and purpose;
- the prompts or interaction category;
- what was independently verified;
- what was revised or rejected;
- the student's own analytical contribution.

## Formative assessment

The practice activities prepare you for graded work by providing opportunities for feedback on:

- one observation versus interpretation exercise;
- one dependency-mechanism map;
- one decision-premise classification;
- one evidence-boundary statement.

These checks address the most consequential category errors before summative assessment.
