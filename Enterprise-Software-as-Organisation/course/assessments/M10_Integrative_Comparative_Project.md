# Integrative comparative project: draft assignment brief

**Postgraduate master course · Provisional summative component · Programme outcomes 11 and 12**

Analyse at least two bounded process or architecture configurations. Use a third case as a boundary contrast where it helps, or explain why it would not test the same claim. The goal is to make and defend one useful comparison rather than describe three processes in full. A plausible candidate is a difference in how reference procedures route work, delimit scope, expose results or make a later task depend on an earlier record. Formulate your own question; the Module 10 worked comparison and studio are rehearsals, not submissions to copy.

## Deliverables

- A written analysis of **1,800 to 2,400 words** as a provisional planning range, excluding references and evidence register.
- One comparison matrix and an evidence register with scope, source type, exact locator, observation and access level. Add diagrams only where they aid the argument and supply a text equivalent.
- A short oral defence: **8 minutes plus 5 minutes of questions** as a provisional format. For a full cohort, schedule separate panels or assessment slots; these defences are not all expected to fit within the Week 12 rehearsal.

State the selected process windows, edition, localisation and release or retrieval context. Explain why the cases are comparable on your question. Reconstruct the relevant branches before comparing them. Use two theoretical lenses where each does identifiable analytical work. Test at least one rival and one contrast or negative case, and say which observation would change the conclusion. Explicitly separate what is documented, interpreted, unknown, and outside the scope of the claim.

Source access need not be identical across students. A controlled course dossier can support the assignment if it contains sufficient evidence for the question. Distinguish what you read firsthand from mediated cards or published summaries. Do not include restricted copies. If institutionally permitted, disclose AI assistance and what you verified independently. The written and oral arguments must be your own.

## Marking guidance

The [assessment strategy](../04_Assessment_Strategy.md) provisionally assigns this component 35% of the course. The dimensions below apply within the component and do not change the course weighting.

| Dimension | Indicative weight | What a strong submission shows |
|---|---:|---|
| Conceptual accuracy | 20% | Defines the operation and theoretical terms without flattening case differences. |
| Evidence quality and traceability | 20% | Lets a reader find and verify every consequential observation at the stated access level. |
| Analytical reasoning | 25% | Shows how evidence supports a bounded relationship rather than relying on feature names or similarities alone. |
| Rivals and boundaries | 15% | Tests a plausible rival, uses scope-aware counter-evidence and distinguishes uncertainty from absence. |
| Integration and originality | 10% | Makes an independently argued comparative claim or a justified rejection of one. |
| Communication | 10% | Uses a readable matrix and defends the claim clearly, including in response to a challenge. |

**Defence questions:** What is your strongest source step? Which case most threatens the claim? What would you need to make a claim about implementation or practice? What evidence would change your mind?

Institutional approval, submission rules, accommodations, moderation, and cohort-specific timing remain to be agreed. This is a draft brief, not an approved examination or grading schedule.
