# Draft briefs: analytical portfolio, theory comparison and process analysis

**Postgraduate master course · Provisional planning drafts, not institutional assessment instructions**

These three briefs prepare for the [independent comparative project](M10_Integrative_Comparative_Project.md). The weights below follow the [assessment strategy](../04_Assessment_Strategy.md). Dates, lengths, marking moderation, accommodations and permitted assistance must be confirmed before delivery. The published instructor examples are practice aids, not answers to submit.

## 1. Weekly analytical portfolio · 20%

Collect notes from weekly exercises, then choose **four revised entries**: an evidence annotation or process reconstruction (Modules 1 or 2), a dependency and mechanism analysis (Modules 3 or 4), an information or knowledge-boundary analysis (Modules 5 or 6), and a decision, control or implementation analysis (Modules 7 to 9). Include one artefact per entry, such as a map or decision table, and a short explanation of what changed after feedback. Use the original exercise guidance for length; as a planning range allow 120 to 250 words per entry, plus a 150 to 250 word reflection across the portfolio.

Assess evidence location, precision of the distinction being made, and whether the revision addresses a genuine overclaim or uncertainty. An unresolved question supported by good reasoning is preferable to a confident assertion the source cannot sustain. Core assessed outcomes are 2 to 10 across the portfolio design; an individual entry need not cover them all. Introductory and later exercises also rehearse outcomes 1, 11 and 12.

## 2. Theory comparison paper · 20%

Choose **one organisational problem** and compare two theories that ask meaningfully different questions about it. For example, a documented handoff could be examined as a dependency-management problem and as a knowledge-boundary problem. Define the theories, show how each changes the analysis of the same evidence, name a limitation of each and explain whether the explanations complement or compete with one another. Support the theory claims with verified scholarly sources and distinguish a hypothetical application from a documentary finding.

**Planning range:** 1,200 to 1,600 words. Suggested point for formative feedback: after Module 6. Assess conceptual accuracy, the quality of the comparison and the evidence boundary, not the number of authors cited. Primary outcomes: 1, 4, 5 and 7.

## 3. Process architecture analysis · 25%

Select **one bounded process window** and formulate a question about how an arrangement is represented. Submit a short evidence register, a labelled process or mechanism map, and an analysis of one normal and one optional or exceptional path where the available evidence permits. Use exact locators. Explain one dependency, one consequential operation and one plausible rival. State what is not shown and what source could resolve it. If the dossier does not cover an exception, do not invent one; narrow the question or propose a clearly labelled evidence search.

**Planning range:** 1,400 to 1,800 words, excluding references and evidence register. Suggested point for formative feedback: after Module 8. Assess the reconstruction, traceability, reasoning and scope. Primary outcomes: 3 to 10 and 12. The capstone later requires comparison across configurations; it is not a resubmission of this analysis.

## Shared submission rules

Identify what was read firsthand and what came from a mediated course card. Include a text equivalent for diagrams. Use the [common marking dimensions](../04_Assessment_Strategy.md#core-marking-dimensions). Follow the eventual institution's rules on collaboration and AI assistance and disclose permitted use.
