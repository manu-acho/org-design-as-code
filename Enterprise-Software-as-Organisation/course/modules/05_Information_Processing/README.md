## Organisational problem

Interdependent and uncertain work creates information requirements. The module examines how organisations reduce information needs or increase their capacity to create, transmit, interpret, and act on information.

## Learning outcomes

After working through this module, you can:

1. explain information-processing requirements and capacity;
2. distinguish information availability, visibility, interpretation, and ability to act;
3. analyse how statuses, alerts, reports, and shared data may direct attention;
4. identify access restrictions and information asymmetries;
5. avoid treating data integration as organisational knowledge.

## How this module fits

Start from the dependency map in [Module 4](../04_Coordination_and_Interdependence/README.md). Module 5 asks how information becomes visible, meaningful and actionable. [Module 6](../06_Knowledge_Boundaries/README.md) examines what happens when groups give the same information different meanings.

The [Stream 1 review](../../../literature/Stream_01_Organisation_Design_Detailed_Review.md#5-information-processing-in-contemporary-organisation-design) supplies the theory, and the [analytical framework](../../../09_Analytical_Framework.md) supplies the evidence boundaries. The optional TOGAF reading supports a small baseline-target-gap exercise, not a second syllabus. No live SAP access is needed. Broader coverage of attention, overload and visibility remains a future literature decision, not a claim of completeness.
