# Postgraduate Master Curriculum Map

## Course architecture

The master course contains ten modules scheduled across twelve weeks. Modules 3 and 4 receive two weeks because they establish the central analytical vocabulary. Module 10 occupies the final week for synthesis and defence rehearsal; formal individual defences are scheduled separately if needed.

| Week | Module | Central question | Principal concepts | Main applied activity |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1. Enterprise Software and Organisation | What becomes visible when software is treated as an organisational proposal? | encoded, implemented, enacted; architecture; organisational primitive | annotate a reference artefact and bound possible claims |
| 2 | 2. End-to-End Processes | How is cross-functional work represented as a process? | process boundary, activity, actor, object, event, outcome | reconstruct a process from controlled documentation |
| 3 | 3. Organisation Design I | How are work and responsibility divided? | differentiation, task division, structure, allocation | identify structural distinctions in a process |
| 4 | 3. Organisation Design II | How are divided activities reintegrated? | integration, configuration, fit, modularity | compare isolated features with a mechanism configuration |
| 5 | 4. Coordination and Interdependence I | What kind of dependency requires management? | pooled, sequential, reciprocal, flow, shared resource, fit | create a dependency map |
| 6 | 4. Coordination and Interdependence II | What mechanism addresses the dependency? | mechanism, accountability, predictability, common understanding, exception | create a dependency-mechanism-condition map |
| 7 | 5. Information Processing | What information must be processed, by whom, and for what action? | uncertainty, information need, capacity, visibility, attention | analyse an information path and its action rights |
| 8 | 6. Knowledge Boundaries | When is shared data insufficient for coordination? | transfer, translation, transformation, boundary object, expertise | analyse one shared object across functional perspectives |
| 9 | 7. Decision Theory | How does architecture shape possible choices? | bounded rationality, premises, programmes, defaults, thresholds, discretion | trace a decision programme and locate judgement |
| 10 | 8. Governance and Control | Who may decide, act, monitor, override, and answer? | permission, responsibility, authority, accountability, control | analyse an approval, override, and audit trace |
| 11 | 9. SAP Activate and Transformation | How does reference design enter implementation choice? | Fit-to-Standard, configuration, scope, standardisation, local variation | run a Fit-to-Standard simulation and hand decisions, backlog and tests to delivery |
| 12 | 10. Comparative Process Analysis | What patterns recur, vary, or remain unsupported across processes? | configuration comparison, rival explanation, negative case, boundary | run a three-case comparative studio and rehearse the independent project defence |

## Concept progression

The sequence is cumulative:

1. Enterprise software represents part of an organisational arrangement; the artefact is not the enacted organisation.
2. End-to-end process reconstruction makes roles, objects, boundaries, handoffs and alternatives inspectable.
3. Organisation design explains division and integration.
4. Coordination theory identifies dependencies and connects them to proposed mechanisms and conditions.
5. Information-processing theory explains informational demand and capacity.
6. Knowledge-boundary research explains why common data may not create common meaning.
7. Decision theory explains how premises, programmes, and discretion shape choice.
8. Governance and control distinguish legitimate rights, monitoring, accountability, and consequences.
9. SAP Activate places encoded arrangements within implementation and transformation choices.
10. Comparative analysis tests whether candidate patterns recur across process settings.

## Process cases

The preferred process sequence is:

- P2P as the initial case for sequential dependency, matching, approval, and exception handling;
- O2C for customer-facing coordination, fulfilment, credit, billing, and hand-offs;
- R2R for shared representations, consolidation, control, close, and reporting;
- Recruit to Retire when its corpus is sufficiently comparable for role, information, authority, and lifecycle analysis.

The bounded Swiss J45, BD9 and J58 corpus supports the drafted cases. The [student route](START_HERE.md#why-cases-recur) explains how the course reuses them for different questions. Recruit to Retire is an orientation topic, not an equivalent documentary case.

## How to use the reading packs

The reading packs guide you through:

- one **orientation reading** written for accessibility;
- one or two **essential scholarly readings**;
- one **application or boundary reading**;
- controlled SAP artefacts for the applied activity;
- optional advanced readings.

The packs indicate whether the source was checked in full text, in abstract, through bibliographic details, or at another access level.

## What you will find in a module

Start with the module overview for the topic, learning outcomes and any earlier concepts it builds on. The lesson and reading pack introduce the theory. A documentary case or clearly labelled illustration gives you something to analyse. Use the seminar questions, exercise and checks for understanding to practise before working on the related assessment. Worked guidance can help you compare your reasoning after your first attempt.
