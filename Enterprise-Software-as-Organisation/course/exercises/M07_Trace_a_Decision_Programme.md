# Exercise DT-EX-01: Trace a decision before and after the click

**Module 7 · Postgraduate · Formative portfolio practice**

**Preparation:** Read the [lesson](../modules/07_Decision_Theory/01_Student_Lesson.md) and the [case cards and fictional brief](../cases/M07_BD9_Routing_and_Decision.md). **In class:** 35 minutes. No live SAP access is required.

1. **Trace the documentary programme.** Identify the recorded input, the configured comparison, the represented routing and the reviewer's choices. Cite the evidence IDs and locators. Give one reason why an unconditioned workflow is not automatic approval and one reason why a rework option is not evidence of an override.
2. **Run the fictional policy on paper.** Write the expected amount route for each order. Identify a factual premise, a value premise and the operational rule. For D, E and F, state what the rule leaves unresolved. Keep the route and the eventual decision in separate columns.
3. **Design for the exception.** Compare a specialist recommendation with an independent specialist decision. Which is closer to the client's needs, and what would you have to find out to recommend it? Name who owns the policy and who should decide whether changed orders need review again. If these are open questions, say so.
4. **Write a baseline-target-gap row and tests.** Include a threshold boundary check, an unusual order with a known or missing trigger, and a negative check for a user without the required permission. State one result that would support correct implementation and one source of evidence you would still need to assess actual authority.

**In class:** Map the decision programme and sketch expected routes and tests. **After class, submit:** A premise-to-action diagram or table with a text equivalent, a short test matrix, and 180 to 250 words of reasoning. Label documented observations, fictional facts and proposed designs.

**Short transfer check, after class:** Use the lesson's fictional clearing example. Classify two cases, one eligible and one ambiguous. For each, name the expected state, the evidence you would inspect and who can resolve an error. Also give one check that distinguishes an editable preselection from a binding value. This extends the portfolio reflection; it does not add a graded component.

| Criterion | A strong answer |
|---|---|
| Premises and programme | Separates the observed value, the policy objective and the rule that operationalises it. |
| Documentary reasoning | Distinguishes setup, routing, reviewer choice, rework and resulting state. |
| Authority and discretion | Explains who may advise, decide and act; does not infer effective authority from a log. |
| Verification | Tests a boundary and an exception, gives expected results where the policy is agreed, and flags unresolved policy. |

The exercise rehearses outcomes 2, 3, 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12 for the [weekly analytical portfolio](../04_Assessment_Strategy.md#weekly-analytical-portfolio).
