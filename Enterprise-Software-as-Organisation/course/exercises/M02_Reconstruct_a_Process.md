# Exercise EP-EX-01: Draw only the arrows you can defend

**Module 2 · Week 2 · Formative process laboratory**

**Preparation:** Read the [lesson](../modules/02_End_to_End_Processes/01_Student_Lesson.md) and [dossier](../cases/M02_J45_and_BD9_Process_Reconstruction.md). Start with the J45 worked example. Use the BD9 cards to do your own reconstruction. No SAP account or modelling software is required.

| Stage | Time | Task |
|---|---:|---|
| Bound the case | 5 min | Begin with delivery creation using an earlier sales document. End at the saved billing document. Name what precedes and succeeds the window without drawing those processes in full. |
| Build the map | 12 min | For B1, B2 and B5, identify actor or represented function, action, object and expected state. Label each arrow as documented sequence, record reuse or an unverified relation. |
| Add variation | 8 min | Show B3 or B4, with its stated optionality and what changes. Explain what claim about delivery cardinality or mandatory confirmation this limits. |
| Challenge a claim | 5 min | Rewrite “once goods issue is posted, the customer has received the goods and the invoice will be paid”. State what further evidence would be needed. |

**In class:** Sketch the map and draft evidence rows. **After class, submit:** A labelled map or equivalent table, three or more evidence rows, and 120 to 180 words explaining one supported link, one uncertainty and one out-of-scope claim. If you used the cards rather than an original, identify the source as a mediated summary. This rehearses programme outcomes 2, 3, 4 and 12 in the [weekly analytical portfolio](../04_Assessment_Strategy.md#weekly-analytical-portfolio).

**Extension:** Compare the optional split with a genuine discrepancy in one of the cases. Explain why a different route does not necessarily indicate a failure. Do not infer an error or a corrective decision from the word “split”.
