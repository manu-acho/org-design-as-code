# Exercise CO-EX-01: Map a dependency, a mechanism and a condition

**Module 4 · Postgraduate · Formative portfolio practice**

**Preparation:** 15 minutes with the [BD9 evidence cards](../cases/M04_BD9_Delivery_and_Billing.md). **In class:** 30 minutes. Begin individually and test your reasoning with a partner. No SAP account is required. Read [Part I of the lesson](../modules/04_Coordination_and_Interdependence/01_Student_Lesson.md#part-i-identify-the-dependency-before-choosing-a-mechanism) and the mechanism-condition distinction in Part II first.

1. **Map both cards separately.** Identify the earlier activity or record, the later task, what the task takes from the record, and the documented consequence. Cite the case evidence ID and step range. Mark what is optional or expected.
2. **Name the problem and mechanism.** Explain why selecting work for delivery creation is not identical to supplying a quantity to billing. For either route, propose one alternative mechanism that addresses the same dependency, labelled as hypothetical.
3. **Test the rival.** A colleague says the record is merely a lookup aid. Explain where that reading fits, where it is insufficient, and what source detail discriminates between the two readings.
4. **Separate condition from result.** State one condition the mechanism might support, such as predictability. Name one way to check that condition in practice and one different measure you would need to investigate performance. Do not claim either was observed in the case.

**In class:** Draft the two maps and discuss a rival. **After class, submit:** A two-row matrix or two annotated diagrams, a 150 to 220 word explanation and a one-sentence boundary statement. Provide a brief text equivalent if you draw the diagrams. Identify which sources you accessed and distinguish your own checks from any permitted assistance.

| Feedback criterion | A strong answer |
|---|---|
| Dependency and traceability | Names two connected activities or an earlier record and its use, not just a sequence. |
| Mechanism | Shows what the rule, selection input or quantity does on the named route. |
| Rival and optionality | Uses specific evidence to qualify a claim and keeps the routes separate. |
| Condition and evidence boundary | Distinguishes what the procedure represents from what needs observation and performance data. |

This exercise rehearses programme outcomes 2 to 6, 11 and 12 for the [weekly analytical portfolio](../04_Assessment_Strategy.md#weekly-analytical-portfolio). It does not add a graded component.
