# Exercise OD-EX-01: Compare two arrangements for invoice posting

**Module 3 · Postgraduate · Formative portfolio practice**

**Preparation:** 15 minutes with the [case evidence cards](../cases/M03_J45_Invoice_Routes.md), separate from lesson reading. **In class:** 30 minutes to draft and compare in pairs. **After class:** Submit your own revised answer. No SAP account or live system is needed.

**Prerequisites:** Module 3, Part I, and the fit distinctions in Part II. **Outcomes:** Compare a design configuration rather than an isolated feature, and distinguish coherence, implementation fit and enacted fit.

## Task

1. **Reconstruct.** For each route record the actor, input and its source, what the procedure checks, and the expected end state. Give the evidence ID and step range. If you used an evidence card rather than the original source, state that.
2. **Compare.** Draw two separate lines from inputs to expected posting. Annotate the earlier purchasing contribution on one route and the accounting inputs on the other. Identify a similarity that does not depend on both routes using the same data.
3. **Challenge.** Someone says, “The PO number is just a convenience. The routes organise work in the same way.” Write the strongest version of that explanation, then identify one fact that supports a more specific interpretation. State what the evidence still cannot decide.
4. **Bound.** Name one supported claim about the represented design. Then propose one source of evidence for implementation fit and one different source for enacted fit. Do not say that either route is better.

## Deliverable

Submit one comparison table or annotated diagram, a **150 to 220 word** explanation, and one sentence identifying the strongest limitation. A diagram must have a short text equivalent. If you use AI assistance under your institution's rules, identify which claims and locators you verified yourself.

## Feedback criteria

| Check | A strong answer does this |
|---|---|
| Reconstruction | Identifies specific inputs, checks and expected states with traceable evidence. |
| Design reasoning | Separates the posted state from the different dependencies and work needed to reach it. |
| Rival interpretation | Explains what a lookup alone could show and what item population adds. |
| Evidence boundary | Does not infer mandatory three-way matching, no controls on the alternative, or actual customer outcomes. |
