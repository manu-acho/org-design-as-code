# Teaching and Evidence Standard

## Purpose

This standard ensures that course accessibility does not weaken scholarly accuracy or the project's evidential boundaries. It applies to lessons, slides, cases, exercises, assessments, instructor notes, and pathway adaptations.

## Source hierarchy

Use sources in the following order of authority for the claim being made:

1. verified original scholarly work for theoretical claims;
2. official, release-controlled SAP material for represented product or process arrangements;
3. peer-reviewed empirical research for implementation and practice claims;
4. verified secondary syntheses for orientation and comparison;
5. certification notes as learning aids whose claims remain subject to source verification.

Discovery reports, search summaries, generated text, and unidentified uploads may identify candidates. They are not evidence unless their claims are independently verified.

## Mandatory labels for examples

Every example must be identifiable as one of the following:

- **Verified documentary example:** Supported by a registered source artefact.
- **Verified empirical example:** Supported by an identified empirical study or project evidence.
- **Illustrative example:** Constructed to explain a concept and not presented as a finding.
- **Hypothetical scenario:** Constructed to explore alternatives or consequences.
- **Vendor claim:** A claim made by the vendor and attributed as such.

## Case provenance

Every SAP case must record:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Case ID | Stable identifier |
| Process and scope | Exact process boundary and relevant solution-process ID when available |
| Product and deployment | Exact product and deployment model |
| Release or retrieval date | Version context for changeable material |
| Source type | Official help, process flow, test script, role documentation, configuration documentation, or other type |
| Source location | URL, file, page, section, or artefact identifier |
| Observation | What the source directly represents |
| Interpretation | The proposed organisational meaning |
| Rival interpretation | At least one plausible alternative |
| Evidence boundary | What the source cannot establish |
| Verification status | Full text, abstract, metadata, pending, or excluded |

## Writing standard

Course content inherits `../literature/WRITING_STANDARD.md`. In particular:

- begin with the practical or organisational problem;
- define specialised terms on first use;
- explain why each theorist or concept enters the discussion;
- connect authors instead of listing them;
- distinguish definition, illustration, research use, and boundary;
- use one main intellectual job per paragraph;
- keep citations close to supported claims;
- do not use em dashes.

## Inferential discipline

Teaching material must not convert:

- capability into use;
- permission into authority;
- assignment into accountability;
- shared data into shared understanding;
- workflow into successful coordination;
- automation into elimination of discretion;
- process documentation into enacted routine;
- vendor benefits into demonstrated outcomes;
- a standard flow into the complete organisational architecture.

## Student-facing transparency

Students should be told when:

- a source was available only as an abstract or metadata record;
- a case is illustrative rather than empirical;
- a proposition remains untested;
- a process or feature may vary by release, deployment model, scope, or configuration;
- the available material cannot answer a question about practice or performance.

## Content review checklist

Before a unit is marked teaching-ready, confirm:

- [ ] learning outcomes are observable and assessable;
- [ ] content is aligned with those outcomes;
- [ ] theoretical claims have verified sources;
- [ ] SAP claims have controlled provenance;
- [ ] all examples carry the correct status;
- [ ] observations and interpretations are separate;
- [ ] rival interpretations are included where material;
- [ ] encoded, implemented, and enacted levels are not conflated;
- [ ] readings have access and verification records;
- [ ] exercises include guidance or a defensible solution route;
- [ ] instructor notes identify misconceptions;
- [ ] accessibility and formatting checks are complete;
- [ ] no em dashes are present.
