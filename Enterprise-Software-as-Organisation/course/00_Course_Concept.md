# Course Concept

## Working title

**Enterprise Software as Organisation Design: Processes, Coordination and Digital Transformation**

## Course proposition

Enterprise software can be studied as a codified proposal for organising work. Its architecture represents activities, roles, objects, sequences, information relationships, permissions, decisions, controls, exceptions, and escalation possibilities. These representations matter organisationally, but they do not determine how a customer configures or enacts them.

## Academic level and format

The master course is designed for postgraduate study at MSc, MBA, Executive Master, or equivalent advanced professional level.

Provisional format:

- 12 teaching weeks;
- 10 content modules;
- one weekly seminar or lecture-seminar;
- one applied laboratory, workshop, or guided analytical activity;
- 7.5 ECTS as the default design assumption, adjustable to institutional requirements;
- approximately 187.5 total student-learning hours at 7.5 ECTS;
- a balance of theory, documentary analysis, process knowledge, and integrative application.

Credit value, contact hours, prerequisites, and institutional level descriptors remain provisional until a target programme is selected.

## Educational purpose

The course enables students to move beyond two narrow views of enterprise software: software as a technical platform and software as a collection of business-process instructions. Students learn to examine the organisational arrangements represented in enterprise architecture and to make claims that are theoretically precise and evidentially bounded.

## Distinctive contribution

The course combines five elements that are commonly taught separately:

1. end-to-end process understanding;
2. organisation design and coordination theory;
3. decision, authority, governance, and control;
4. enterprise-system architecture and transformation;
5. rigorous analysis of vendor and process artefacts.

The course therefore trains students to ask not only how a process operates, but also what organisational design the process presupposes, enables, or constrains.

## Scope

The master course covers:

- enterprise software as an organisational research object;
- end-to-end process architecture;
- differentiation, integration, and interdependence;
- coordination mechanisms and integrative conditions;
- information-processing requirements and capacity;
- knowledge boundaries and shared representations;
- bounded rationality, decision premises, programmes, and discretion;
- authority, permission, responsibility, governance, and accountability;
- standardisation, exception handling, and escalation;
- SAP Activate and Fit-to-Standard as transformation settings;
- comparative analysis across P2P, O2C, R2R, and, when evidence permits, Recruit to Retire.

## Exclusions

The course is not intended to provide:

- transaction-level SAP configuration training;
- comprehensive preparation for an SAP certification examination;
- claims about customer behaviour based only on reference documentation;
- vendor-benefit claims without independent evidence;
- a general survey of every ERP platform;
- implementation recommendations unsupported by organisational context.

## Pedagogical sequence

Each unit should move through five stages:

1. **Organisational problem:** Begin with a recognisable problem of divided and interdependent work.
2. **Concrete representation:** Use a process, role, object, rule, workflow, or exception to make the problem visible.
3. **Theoretical explanation:** Introduce the concepts and authors needed to interpret the representation.
4. **Analytical return:** Revisit the artefact using more precise language and alternative interpretations.
5. **Evidence boundary:** State what the artefact establishes and what would require implementation or practice evidence.

## Relationship with the research and book

The course, research, and book share verified intellectual and empirical infrastructure but have different purposes:

| Output | Primary purpose | Organising logic |
|---|---|---|
| Research papers | Produce focused theoretical and empirical contributions | Research question and evidence |
| Book | Develop a sustained scholarly argument for a broad academic and practitioner audience | Cumulative argument |
| Course | Produce learning and demonstrable capability | Concept progression and constructive alignment |

Teaching may expose unclear concepts or weak examples, but the course cannot be used as evidence for research findings. Research results may enter teaching only after they satisfy the project's evidential requirements.

## Development principle

The postgraduate course is the master design. Undergraduate and executive versions must cite the master module from which content was derived and record any change in learning outcome, reading depth, activity, assessment, or claim scope.
