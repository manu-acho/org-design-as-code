# Course Literature Crosswalk and Development Gates

## Purpose

This crosswalk prevents the course from creating a second literature review. The research literature map remains the authoritative map of theoretical streams. Course modules draw from those streams and add pedagogical packaging, activities, and assessment. A new source enters the shared bibliography and literature infrastructure before it enters a course reading pack.

## Three kinds of source need

The course requires three source families that must remain separate:

1. **Substantive scholarship:** Theory and empirical research used to explain enterprise software organisationally. This belongs in `../03_Literature_Map.md`, `../literature/`, and `../references/`.
2. **SAP and process evidence:** Release-controlled artefacts used for cases and exercises. This belongs in the shared corpus and evidence register under `../data/`.
3. **Teaching-design literature:** Sources on constructive alignment, case teaching, assessment, accessibility, and learning design. These govern pedagogy but do not belong in the substantive literature review unless they contribute to the research question.

## Relationship to the nine-stream literature map

The three coverage gaps identified through course design do not create three additional reviews beyond the original eight. They are resolved as follows:

- packaged enterprise-system implementation and transformation expands Stream 4;
- governance, control, accountability, and decision rights expands Stream 8;
- business-process management and process architecture becomes the new Stream 9.

The authoritative programme therefore contains nine substantive streams.

## Shared literature work completed for course theory

### Completed priority streams

1. **Stream 9: Business-process management and process architecture**  
   Completed for Module 2. The review clarifies process, event, activity, actor, object, state, boundary, hand-off, variation, exception, architecture, and the distinctions among process models, event logs, and performed work.

2. **Expanded Stream 8: Governance, organisational control, accountability, and decision rights**  
   Completed for Module 8 and the research coding framework. The review distinguishes behaviour and output control, control portfolios, monitoring, standards, intervention, consequences, decision rights, formal and real authority, answerability, traceability, and legitimacy.

### Completed expansion

**Expanded Stream 4: Enterprise systems, implementation, and transformation**
Completed for Modules 1 and 9, the book, and later papers. The detailed review now covers integration, lifecycle, multidimensional fit, learning, adaptation, configuration, customisation, outcomes, and the limits of Fit-to-Standard claims. Independent evidence on contemporary public-cloud ERP remains limited and requires continuing retrieval rather than unsupported inference.

### Priority B: valuable consolidation, but current teaching can begin

4. **Routines, sociomateriality, and technology-in-practice**  
   Completed through Streams 5 and 6. The shared reviews now distinguish artefacts, recurrent performances, material arrangements, relational possibilities, actualisation, situated practice, and outcomes. Module development should reuse these reviews rather than create a parallel synthesis.

5. **Information, attention, and organisational visibility**  
   Galbraith and the completed streams support an initial module. A later supplement may be needed to cover attention allocation, information visibility, overload, selective representation, and the ability to act on information.

6. **Digital infrastructure, standards, and classification**  
   Completed through Stream 7. Module development should use its distinctions among bounded artefact, infrastructure, installed base, standards, classification, generativity, and control points.

### Course-only teaching literature

The course also needs a small, separately governed teaching-design bibliography covering:

- constructive alignment;
- learning taxonomies and outcome design;
- case-based and problem-based learning;
- authentic assessment;
- rubric design and moderation;
- inclusive and accessible course design;
- responsible use of generative AI in assessment.

These sources should be stored in a future teaching-design register under `course/`. They should not be mixed into the substantive organisation and enterprise-systems literature review.

## Non-duplication workflow

For every new module:

1. identify its rows in the shared literature map;
2. use the existing detailed review and source assessment where available;
3. record missing concepts in this crosswalk;
4. add genuinely new sources once to the shared bibliography and literature matrix;
5. update or create one shared detailed review;
6. derive the student lesson, instructor explanation, and reading route from that review;
7. keep SAP evidence in the shared corpus and reference it by ID from the teaching case;
8. do not reproduce source audits inside the course folder.

## Review trigger

Update this crosswalk whenever:

- a literature stream is completed;
- a module moves from scaffold to draft or teaching-ready;
- the SAP release or process scope is frozen;
- a case is registered;
- a new course pathway changes the required depth of a stream.
