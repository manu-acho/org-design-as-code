# Reuse and Publication Map

## Principle

The project may reuse a common evidence base, constructs, and analytical infrastructure across outputs. It should not reuse substantial prose, figures, tables, or argument structures without disclosure, permission where required, and publisher-policy review.

## Output differentiation

| Output | Core job | Distinctive contribution | Book relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dissertation/research project | Establish and defend the complete academic study | Theory, method, evidence, limitations, and contribution under examination requirements | Research foundation; not a book manuscript |
| Paper 1 | Introduce encoded organisation design and validate the documentary method | Focused theoretical-methodological contribution | Condensed source for Chapters 1–3, rewritten and extended |
| Process papers | Make bounded empirical contributions | Deep analysis of one process or mechanism | Evidence source for process chapters, not pasted chapters |
| Comparative paper | Explain cross-process similarities and differences | Comparative theoretical contribution | Basis for Chapters 13–14, expanded for broader readership |
| Book | Integrate theory, method, cases, comparison, and transformation implications | Coherent interdisciplinary account for academic and reflective-practitioner audiences | Independent narrative and synthesis |

## Reuse register fields

Before drafting overlapping material, record:

- output and exact section;
- originating manuscript/publication;
- publication status and rights holder;
- type of reuse: idea, data, prose, table, figure, or instrument;
- amount of overlap;
- disclosure/citation required;
- permission required and status;
- rewritten or extended contribution;
- editor or publisher guidance.

## High-risk areas

- Reusing article introductions, literature-review prose, or discussion sections verbatim.
- Republishing a journal figure or table without checking licence and contract terms.
- Describing already published analysis as wholly new.
- Submitting materially overlapping manuscripts without disclosure.
- Reusing SAP screenshots, diagrams, or proprietary text without permission.
- Allowing book deadlines to cause premature empirical claims in the articles or dissertation.

## Drafting policy

Book chapters should be written for their own audience and argumentative role. They may cite related publications and reuse verified evidence through fresh synthesis. A live reuse register should be established before any chapter text derived from submitted or published work is drafted.
