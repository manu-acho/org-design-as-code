# Book Concept

## Working title

**Enterprise Software as Organisation Design: Coordination, Processes and Transformation in SAP S/4HANA**

## One-sentence proposition

Enterprise software can be studied as a codified organisation-design proposal because its reference architecture represents how work is divided, dependencies are managed, responsibilities and decision opportunities are allocated, information is made visible, and normal and exceptional activity should proceed.

## Book argument

Research and practice commonly approach enterprise software through implementation, adoption, technical integration, or post-implementation effects. Those perspectives are indispensable, but they often leave the organisational content of the reference architecture underanalysed. Before any customer configures or enacts the system, reference artefacts already specify process sequences, business objects, roles, approvals, states, controls, information relationships, and exception possibilities.

The book develops a disciplined way to reconstruct that encoded organisational logic. Organisation design explains interdependence and broad coordination choices. Coordination theory connects dependencies with mechanisms and artefacts. Decision and control theory clarify authority, decision premises, monitoring, and discretion. Routines, sociomateriality, and infrastructure research establish the limits of documentary inference. SAP S/4HANA supplies a consequential empirical setting in which these ideas can be examined across end-to-end processes.

The book does not argue that software determines an organisation. It distinguishes encoded logic from implemented configuration and enacted practice. The empirical contribution is therefore a reconstruction of documented organisational possibilities and constraints - not a claim that customer organisations adopt them unchanged or achieve particular outcomes.

## Intended format

- Research monograph with practical relevance, not an SAP configuration manual.
- Provisional length: 80,000–100,000 words, subject to publisher discussion.
- Four parts: reframing, theoretical foundations, process analyses, and comparative/transformation implications.
- Primary case: a frozen release and deployment model of SAP S/4HANA.
- Core comparative processes: Procure to Pay, Order to Cash, Record to Report, and, if the corpus supports comparability, Recruit to Retire.

## Expository method

The book should be pleasurable to read without sacrificing scholarly precision. Each chapter will move through a teaching sequence: a concrete organisational problem, an illustrative process or artefact, the theory needed to interpret it, a return to the case, and an explicit statement of evidence limits. Relationships among theorists will be explained rather than left as citation sequences. Definitions, examples, research applications, and boundaries will be made visible to the reader.

Detailed distinctions, counterarguments, propositions, and verification notes must be retained in the chapter, a table, a note, or an appendix. The shared [Scholarly Writing and Teaching Standard](../literature/WRITING_STANDARD.md) controls manuscript drafting.

## Distinctive contribution

1. Treats enterprise-software reference architecture as an organisational research object in its own right.
2. Provides a reproducible documentary method for moving from software artefacts to bounded organisation-theory interpretations.
3. Develops and evaluates software organisational primitives across end-to-end processes.
4. Explains how coordination, governance, information processing, control, and exception handling intersect without collapsing them.
5. Connects encoded arrangements to transformation choices while refusing deterministic claims about implementation or practice.

## Boundary conditions

- SAP documentation supports claims about represented or permitted arrangements, not actual customer practices.
- Deployment models and releases are not interchangeable.
- Standard flows cannot stand for the complete architecture; exceptions and negative cases are required.
- Shared objects do not establish shared understanding.
- Permissions and approvals do not by themselves establish legitimate or exercised authority.
- Vendor descriptions of benefits do not establish performance effects.

## Provisional audiences

Researchers and advanced students in information systems, organisation studies, business-process management, enterprise architecture, and digital transformation; transformation leaders, process owners, enterprise architects, and experienced SAP practitioners seeking a theoretically grounded interpretation of enterprise systems.

## Success criterion

The book succeeds if readers can examine an enterprise-software artefact and make a precise, evidence-bounded statement about the organisational arrangement it encodes, the theory that explains it, plausible rival interpretations, and what additional evidence would be required to claim implementation or enacted effects.
