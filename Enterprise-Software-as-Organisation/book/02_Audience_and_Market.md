# Audience and Market

## Primary audience

The primary audience is researchers, graduate students, and reflective practitioners working at the intersection of enterprise systems and organisation. They need a vocabulary and method for understanding enterprise software as more than technology or an implementation project.

## Reader groups and needs

| Reader | Need | Book response |
|---|---|---|
| Information-systems researchers | A theoretically defensible way to analyse reference architecture | Reproducible documentary method and encoded/implemented/enacted boundary |
| Organisation scholars | Access to the organisational content of enterprise systems | Translation of processes, roles, objects, rules, and controls into bounded theoretical constructs |
| BPM and enterprise-architecture students | A bridge between process models and organisation theory | End-to-end cases and comparative analytical displays |
| Transformation leaders and process owners | A way to identify organisational choices hidden in “standard” software | Fit-to-Standard, decision-right, exception, and visibility questions |
| Enterprise architects and SAP practitioners | A non-technical account of organisational consequences and trade-offs | Theory-led interpretation without pretending architecture determines outcomes |

## Positioning statement

For readers who design, study, or transform organisations through enterprise systems, this book provides a theory-led and empirically traceable method for revealing the organisational arrangements encoded in software. Unlike implementation manuals or general enterprise-systems textbooks, it focuses on the organisational content of reference architecture and distinguishes encoded possibilities from configured and enacted realities.

## Market claims requiring evidence

The proposal must not use unverified claims about market size, course adoption, practitioner demand, SAP installed base, or lack of competing titles. These require current, cited evidence before submission. Interviews or feedback from representative academic and practitioner readers would strengthen the proposition but are not yet available.

## Potential teaching uses

- Graduate seminar in enterprise systems.
- Organisation design and technology course.
- Business-process management course.
- Digital-transformation or enterprise-architecture executive education.
- Doctoral reading on materiality, routines, coordination, and information infrastructure.

These are proposed uses, not evidence of adoption demand.
