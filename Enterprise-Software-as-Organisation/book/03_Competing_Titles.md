# Competing and Complementary Titles

## Assessment rule

The final Springer proposal should compare the proposed book with recent and established titles on audience, problem, theoretical orientation, empirical basis, and format. Comparators should be described fairly. A technical or pedagogical focus is a difference, not a defect.

## Initially verified Springer comparators

| Title | Verified positioning | Relationship to proposed book |
|---|---|---|
| Boris Shishkov, *Designing Enterprise Information Systems: Merging Enterprise Modeling and Software Specification* (Springer, 2020) | Combines enterprise modelling and software specification; includes conceptual background, methodological guidance, and case examples | Close in recognising the enterprise–software relationship, but centred on modelling and specification rather than reconstructing organisation design encoded in a major ERP reference architecture |
| Monique Snoeck, *Enterprise Information Systems Engineering: The MERODE Approach* (Springer, 2014) | Method-oriented treatment of enterprise information-system engineering and model-driven design | Complementary engineering approach; the proposed book centres organisation theory, coordination, governance, and documentary analysis |
| Rainer Weber, *Enterprise Information Systems: Models, Integration, and Operation* (Springer, 2025/2026) | Textbook on transactional and analytical systems, integration, and operation, using systems including SAP | Strong recent pedagogical comparator; the proposed monograph is theory-led, research-oriented, and focused on organisational assumptions rather than system fundamentals |
| Qing Li and Yu-Liu Chen, *Modeling and Analysis of Enterprise and Information Systems: From Requirements to Realization* (Springer, 2009) | Enterprise-modelling methods for diagnosis, process reengineering, and information-system implementation | Broader modelling methodology; the proposed book makes a narrower empirical and theoretical argument about encoded organisation design |

## Proposed differentiation

The book’s defensible distinction is the combination of:

1. organisation and coordination theory as the explanatory core;
2. a release-controlled corpus of vendor reference artefacts;
3. a documented method separating software observation from theoretical inference;
4. comparative end-to-end SAP S/4HANA process analyses; and
5. explicit boundaries between encoded architecture, implementation, and enacted practice.
