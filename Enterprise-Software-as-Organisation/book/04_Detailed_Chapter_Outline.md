# Detailed Chapter Outline

**Status:** Architectural outline. Chapter promises remain provisional until the empirical evidence gates are met.

## Part I  -  Rethinking Enterprise Software

### 1. Enterprise Software as Organisation

Introduces the puzzle: enterprise systems are routinely discussed as technologies implemented in organisations, while their architectures already represent organised activity. Establishes the book’s question, significance, SAP S/4HANA case, and non-determinist position. Distinguishes organisation design encoded in reference artefacts from configuration and use.

### 2. From Digital Tool to Codified Organisation Design

Defines encoded organisational logic and software organisational primitives. Explains why roles, workflows, data objects, rules, approvals, states, and system events can perform organisational operations without constituting the organisation itself. Develops the encoded–implemented–enacted model.

### 3. How to Read an Enterprise System Organisationally

Presents the documentary method: release and scope control, source hierarchy, corpus registration, evidence segmentation, two-stage coding, rival interpretations, confidence, and negative cases. Demonstrates traceability from artefact to observation to theory while specifying what documentary evidence cannot establish.

## Part II  -  Theoretical Foundations

### 4. Organisation Design and Interdependence

Connects Thompson’s interdependence, Galbraith’s information-processing perspective, Mintzberg’s coordination mechanisms, and later organisation-design developments. Produces the vocabulary for asking what dependencies a reference architecture presupposes and how design responses vary with uncertainty and differentiation.

### 5. Coordination, Mechanisms and Artefacts

Develops Malone and Crowston’s dependency–mechanism logic and Okhuysen and Bechky’s integrative conditions. Adds coordination artefacts, knowledge boundaries, relational coordination, and coordinating as situated accomplishment. Prevents shared records, workflow, or roles from being mistaken for successful coordination.

### 6. Decision Rights, Authority and Control

Uses decision and control theory to separate allocation of decision opportunities, formal authorisation, accountability, monitoring, and exercised authority. Establishes coding boundaries among coordination, governance, and control.

### 7. Routines, Infrastructure and Sociomaterial Practice

Uses routines, practice, sociomateriality, classification, and infrastructure research to constrain architectural claims. Explains why artefacts participate in organising while remaining dependent on interpretation, situated action, installed bases, and repair.

## Part III  -  The Organisational Architecture of SAP S/4HANA

### 8. SAP S/4HANA as an Empirical Setting

Describes the frozen product edition, deployment model, release, source types, process architecture, business objects, roles, workflows, master data, analytics, and configuration boundary. Separates SAP’s own terminology and benefit claims from the book’s analysis.

### 9. Procure to Pay: Organising External Resource Acquisition

Reconstructs dependencies from requirement through sourcing, purchasing, receipt, invoice verification, and payment. Examines segregation, approval, document flow, matching, tolerances, blocks, and exceptions. Requires a completed process corpus and evidence-backed mechanism map.

### 10. Order to Cash: Organising Customer Fulfilment

Examines commitments across sales, availability, delivery, billing, and receivables. Focuses on cross-functional hand-offs, shared customer and material representations, sequencing, credit or delivery constraints, and exception management.

### 11. Record to Report: Representing the Financial Enterprise

Examines postings, ledgers, reconciliation, close, reporting, and the relationship between operational events and financial representation. Asks how accountability, temporal closure, classification, visibility, and control are encoded.

### 12. Recruit to Retire: Organising the Employment Relationship

Conditional chapter. If a comparable official corpus is available, examines workforce data, organisational assignment, identity, approvals, lifecycle transitions, information boundaries, and responsibility. The chapter must account for the architectural boundary between SAP S/4HANA and SAP SuccessFactors rather than implying a single homogeneous system.

## Part IV  -  Comparison and Transformation

### 13. The Organisational Primitives of Enterprise Software

Evaluates the candidate primitives emerging across cases: actors and roles, activities, dependencies, objects, states, rules, decision points, controls, exceptions, representations, and system events. Retains only constructs supported by repeated evidence and analytically distinct organisational operations.

### 14. Comparing Coordination Across End-to-End Processes

Compares mechanisms and dependencies within and across processes. Examines recurring architecture, process-specific variation, boundary crossings, and negative cases. Avoids inferring general design principles from simple feature frequency.

### 15. Fit-to-Standard as Organisation Design

Develops the implementation bridge: accepting, configuring, extending, integrating, or rejecting reference arrangements can involve organisation-design choices. This is a theoretical and methodological extension unless implementation evidence is added; it must not be presented as an observed negotiation by default.

### 16. Transformation, Tensions and Unintended Consequences

Examines analytically derived tensions: standardisation and local knowledge, integration and coupling, visibility and surveillance, control and adaptability, global templates and local requirements. Outcome claims require external empirical evidence and will not be inferred from architecture.

### 17. Designing and Governing the Software-Defined Enterprise

Synthesises implications for research and practice. Provides a disciplined set of questions for evaluating enterprise-software design and transformation choices. Identifies future comparative, longitudinal, implementation, and AI-agent research without claiming that the book’s SAP case generalises automatically.
