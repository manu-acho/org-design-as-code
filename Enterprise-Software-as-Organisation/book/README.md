# Book Workspace

## Working title

**Enterprise Software as Organisation Design: Coordination, Processes and Transformation in SAP S/4HANA**

This directory contains the proposal and manuscript architecture for a research monograph. The book shares the project’s verified literature, controlled SAP corpus, evidence register, and coded observations, but it is not a compilation of the dissertation or article manuscripts.

## Book purpose

The book will explain how enterprise-software reference architectures can be examined as codified proposals for organising work. It will connect organisation theory with documented SAP S/4HANA process architecture, while preserving the distinction between:

- encoded organisational logic;
- implemented organisational configuration; and
- enacted organisational practice.

Its intended contribution is an integrated account of dependencies, coordination mechanisms, organisational roles, decision points, shared representations, controls, exceptions, and transformation choices across end-to-end processes.

## Reading experience and teaching value

The book will be rigorous, readable, and teachable. Chapters will begin with a recognisable organisational problem, make it concrete through a process or artefact, introduce theory in the order needed to understand that problem, and then return to the case with greater analytical precision. Technical detail, counterarguments, and evidence limits will be preserved in the main text, tables, notes, or appendices rather than removed.

The [Scholarly Writing and Teaching Standard](../literature/WRITING_STANDARD.md) governs drafting. In particular, chapters must explain relationships among theorists, distinguish examples from findings, and keep encoded architecture separate from implementation and enacted practice.

## Publication gate

Preparing the concept and proposal does not imply that the empirical case is complete or that Springer has accepted the book. A proposal should be submitted only after:

1. the SAP release and process scope are frozen;
2. the pilot corpus and codebook version 1.0 pass their quality checks;
3. at least one end-to-end process analysis is complete;
4. the comparative promise of the book is supported by initial evidence;
5. competing titles, permissions, prior-publication reuse, and trademark language have been reviewed.

## Evidence rule

The book must cite the shared corpus and literature infrastructure rather than create a parallel evidence store. Draft prose may not turn reference architecture into claims about customer behaviour or outcomes. Each empirical chapter must inherit the project’s observation–interpretation separation, provenance requirements, rival interpretations, negative-case search, and confidence judgements.

## Immediate route

Develop the proposal progressively while prioritising the empirical pilot. The next book-specific task is to convert the chapter sketch into proposal-grade summaries after the first process analysis establishes what the corpus can genuinely support.
