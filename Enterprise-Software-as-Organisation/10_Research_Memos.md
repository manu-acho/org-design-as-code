# Example Research Memos

These are worked forms using hypothetical placeholders, not empirical SAP findings.

## Theory memo: status as coordination primitive

**Observation.** `[EVIDENCE ID]` documents a named state and specifies that a later activity becomes available only after that state is reached.

**Interpretation.** The status does more than describe a document; it gates a dependency between differentiated activities. Its operation is `record + signal + gate`.

**Theoretical inference.** The construct may instantiate sequential coordination through standardisation of work and information transfer. Galbraith adds that the compressed state reduces the downstream actor's need to reconstruct prior activity.

**Rival.** The state may be a technical transaction-consistency requirement with no meaningful organisational allocation. Examine the actors, permitted actions, and visibility before retaining the organisational interpretation.

**Next evidence.** Product-help transition rules; process step; role/action restriction; exception behaviour.

## Coding memo: approval versus direct supervision

**Issue.** An artefact labels a step “approval”. This warrants `APPROVAL` descriptively and potentially `GOV_APPROVAL_AUTHORITY`. It does not automatically warrant `COORD_DIRECT_SUPERVISION`, because the approver may be a control function rather than the initiator's hierarchical supervisor.

**Decision rule.** Apply direct supervision only when evidence establishes directive/hierarchical authority over work. Otherwise analyse approval as decision authority and formal control; record the Mintzbergian ambiguity.

## Negative-case memo: configurable bypass of dominant sequence

**Candidate pattern.** `[PROVISIONAL PATTERN]` suggests mandatory workflow gating.

**Negative observation.** `[EVIDENCE ID]` indicates the workflow is optional or has a configured automatic-release condition.

**Effect.** Do not discard the pattern; narrow it from mandatory sequencing to configurable allocation between human authority and rule-based automation. Search all three domains for equivalent options. If optionality is widespread, reframe the finding around bounded discretion.

## Method memo: rolling documentation

**Problem.** A Help Portal page reports “latest” while the process package is release-specific.

**Action.** Record the displayed release and retrieval date, capture the page, and grade it B for historical claims. Locate release-specific documentation. If none exists, qualify the analysis as current-architecture evidence and exclude change-over-time claims.

## Emerging-finding memo: shared objects and pooled coordination

**Observation set.** `[LIST EVIDENCE IDS]` shows multiple differentiated activities using the same governed reference object.

**Candidate interpretation.** The object may act as a primitive for pooled coordination by stabilising common categories and reducing bilateral information reconciliation.

**Counter-evidence required.** Local variants, visibility restrictions, duplicate objects, manual reconciliation, and process-specific reinterpretations.

**Status.** Provisional. Promotion requires evidence of reliance, not merely technical reuse, plus an information-governance account of who creates and changes the object.
