# Abstract

Enterprise software coordinates work partly by specifying which records, decisions and states a later activity can use. Yet a documented reference arrangement is not a customer implementation or evidence of work as performed. This study asks how organisational coordination and governance are encoded in enterprise-software reference architecture. It reconstructs selected procedures in three Swiss SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition reference cases: Procurement of Direct Materials, Sell from Stock, and Accounting and Financial Close. Two-stage documentary analysis identifies what a procedure represents before interpreting its organisational relation, and tests the interpretation against alternative branches and rival explanations. The comparison distinguishes records that supply data from records that identify the target of action; conditions for routing an approval from its consequence; and inspectable output from information or rules that affect a later operation. Scheduled and return routes further show why apparently similar mechanisms must be specified at branch level. The contribution is a relational method for making codified organisation design inspectable without inferring actual authority, implementation fit, coordination effectiveness or organisational outcomes.

**Keywords:** enterprise software, organisation design, coordination, governance, reference architecture, SAP S/4HANA
