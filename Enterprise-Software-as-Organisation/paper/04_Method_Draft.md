# Method

## Case and unit of analysis

The study uses an embedded instrumental case design. SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition is selected because its official reference materials expose process procedures, roles, configuration options and boundaries in a form that can be inspected. The three embedded cases vary the work being coordinated: procurement of direct materials (J45), sale from stock (BD9), and accounting and financial close (J58). Selection is theoretical, not representative. These scope items belong to the broader Procure-to-Pay, Order-to-Cash and Record-to-Report domains, but they neither exhaust those domains nor establish that the same transaction traversed them.

The reference boundary is Switzerland, SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition 2608. The J45 procedure family displays 2608.1; BD9 navigation uses the 2608 parameter, without an independent version badge on the pages inspected; J58 displays 2608. Rolling SAP product documentation is separately labelled and used as context rather than as proof of Swiss configuration. Basic Credit Management (BD6) is supporting material for a delegated BD9 branch, not a fourth case. Outgoing payment (J60) and the succeeding receivables scope (J59) are boundary references, not procedures analysed as part of the corresponding cases.

The unit of analysis is an encoded organisational arrangement: an operation, the differentiated work it relates, and the documented condition or consequence that makes it relevant. Neither the application nor the whole end-to-end process is the default unit. Comparisons can therefore ask whether two superficially similar steps perform the same operation, without assuming equal scope across cases.

The case boundaries determine what counts as a meaningful comparison. The three cases share a vendor, edition, release family and national context, but the underlying work differs. A commercial commitment to a supplier, a delivery task for a customer order and a posting to the general ledger do not present the same coordination problem. Holding the reference environment broadly constant makes it possible to compare the operations used for these different tasks. It does not control for all differences in documentation or configuration. A mechanism might be documented in one case and omitted in another for reasons that have nothing to do with the underlying system. The study therefore uses positive evidence for the relations it reconstructs and records absence more cautiously.

A scope item was treated as a bounded body of documentation, not an empirical unit with a single start and end in practice. Optional branches, alternate execution modes and links to succeeding scope items were kept visible in the reconstruction. When a scope item delegated a step elsewhere, the link established the handoff but did not import the delegated procedure into the comparison. BD6 was examined to clarify what the BD9 credit branch delegates; it did not increase the number of embedded cases or supply a transaction history across them. This boundary rule also prevented the financial close material from being used to complete a sales or purchase transaction merely because all three scopes belong to the same suite.

## Corpus and coding

The primary evidence consists of SAP Signavio Process Navigator scope references and Swiss SAP Best Practices setup and test procedures. Official structural, role, application and authorisation materials help locate or qualify selected claims. The initial register contained 25 artefact records and 26 initial evidence records. A maximum-variation pilot coded 15 of those evidence records, five per case, to test the distinction between descriptions and interpretations. The sample included ordinary progression, authority or control, exceptions or reversal, information or structure, and configuration or timing. It was not intended to estimate prevalence.

The focused case pass produced 59 page-level records: 24 for J45, 18 for BD9 and 17 for J58. These are evidence segments, not independent documents or 59 observations of work in practice. They refine and can overlap with the initial records; the counts must not be added as if they measured one population. The working corpus and targeted review are dated 8 to 10 November 2026. The count of unique sources for a publication supplement is separate from these counts and remains subject to source reconciliation and the publication audit.

Descriptive coding identifies actors, operations, objects, states, rules, classifications, exceptions and controls. Interpretive coding asks how these constructs organise interdependence, information, coordination or governance. A candidate operation is retained only when the relevant source specifies the task or object and shows its use, consequence or constraint. The pilot froze Codebook v1.0 and showed that overview bundles could guide sampling but were usually too broad to support strong inferences. The case analysis therefore returned to exact steps, rules and expected states. An expected result is evidence of what a procedure represents, not evidence that the test was run.

### Locating and recording evidence

The source hierarchy reflects the questions the paper asks. Overviews identify the scope, intended process or relationship to another scope. Test procedures provide a more detailed view of the reference action, including the actor, inputs and expected result. Configuration instructions show what is set up for a selected example, but not necessarily what that example will do in every configuration. Role and master-data pages can establish the available classifications and represented responsibilities. Generic product materials can explain why an operation might be possible without verifying that it occurs under the case conditions. A detailed procedure can accordingly narrow a broad overview without implying that either source is a complete specification. When two examples differ in a value such as an approval threshold, the value is not reconciled by assuming they describe one active rule.

The evidence record preserves the case, source location and scope of the statement before it assigns a theoretical interpretation. The descriptive pass asks what is represented: who is instructed to act, what object is selected or changed, which condition is stated, and what output or state is expected. The interpretive pass asks what this means for differentiated work: whether an input comes from an earlier task, whether a condition allocates a decision, or whether a result is available only for inspection. A record can support several descriptions. For example, an invoice procedure can show both the use of earlier data and a consistency check. The codes retain those aspects without treating them as separate events or claiming that either one is more important in everyday work.

Maximum variation in the pilot concerned the kinds of segments selected, not representativeness of a statistical sample. Taking examples of ordinary work and contrasting them with exceptions, role assignments and configuration choices exposed the risk of coding a familiar feature name too quickly. An overview might suggest that approval belongs to a process, while the detailed script shows when the step applies, what the represented recipient can do and which state is expected to follow. The focused case pass returned to that finer grain. The records make the basis of each inference inspectable without claiming that every official page or available branch was analysed exhaustively.

## Comparison and rival testing

Within each case, mechanisms were reconstructed for specified branches rather than for an assumed single process flow. The comparative pass matched operations across cases, including the use of records, workflow routing, formal return, structural scoping, scheduling, and visibility. Each candidate was then tested against alternative branches and rivals such as technical necessity, transaction consistency, accounting classification, interface convenience and documentary convention. The targeted review reopened 23 Swiss SAP Help pages. Nine propositions were assessed: three were retained, three narrowed and three dropped. The number is a count of drafting decisions, not a measure of how frequently a mechanism occurs.

Cross-cutting material could qualify a claim about architecture, but it could not supply a missing case-specific allocation of access.

### How a comparison survived or failed

The within-case reconstruction came first. It established what each selected record did in its own branch before looking for a parallel elsewhere. The cross-case pass then compared relations, not counts of applications, screens or roles. A comparison could survive despite a different technical implementation if the relevant operation was specified in both cases. Conversely, common terms were insufficient when the operations differed. For instance, release can denote the decision on a sales order, a distinct decision on preliminary billing, or an action on a credit-blocked order described in a neighbouring scope. The comparison did not pool these labels as equivalent votes for a common approval mechanism.

Rival testing was designed to change the retained wording, not merely to list caveats. For each proposed inference the review asked what the most plausible narrower or alternative explanation would be and what evidence would distinguish it. A prior document number may be required for technical consistency. If the subsequent activity uses items from that document or acts on the identified record, that technical explanation does not eliminate an organisational interpretation. It constrains the interpretation to the specific dependency shown. A list filtered by an organisational field is a different case: the filter may select work without restricting who may perform it. Without procedure-specific evidence of a permission check, the proposed access claim does not survive. These decisions separate situations where a rival qualifies a finding from those where it removes the basis for the claim.

An alternative branch was treated as a negative case only against a stated proposition. The no-PO invoice is relevant to the proposed necessity of a PO reference, not to the claim that invoice processing is controlled in some way. Direct posting narrows the proposition that verification precedes all posting; it does not establish how the same entry would behave under a configuration that requires verification. This use of alternatives keeps the comparison at the level of the documentary evidence. A different case in a test script can challenge the claimed universality of a reference dependency without becoming an observed instance of rule avoidance.

The figures serve different roles in the audit trail. Figure 3 makes the movement from procedure to interpretation explicit. Figures 4 to 6 reconstruct selected relations for reading alongside the findings. Their arrows describe only the documented order, input or state consequence identified in the caption. They are not process logs, assertions of causal effect or evidence that one object moved through all the panels. The source locators permit a reader to examine the distinction being drawn and, where warranted, disagree with the proposed interpretation without disputing the entire case reconstruction.

The analysis uses a conservative rule for deciding when to stop tracing. A successor reference identifies an interface unless the same object can be followed into the next procedure. An expected state counts as represented output unless evidence of execution is available. A setup choice counts as a documented option unless the source also ties it to the branch being interpreted. These stopping rules were important where a plausible completion could have been supplied from general product knowledge. Leaving the interface, outcome or configuration unfilled makes the comparison less comprehensive, but it prevents a plausible reconstruction from being mistaken for an observed or specified chain.

Traceability in the manuscript is organised around the evidence segment. A reader can start from a finding or a figure, locate its evidence identifier and follow the associated procedure and step in Table 7. The figure is a reconstruction at that grain, not an additional source. The published locators separate the evidence needed to inspect the claim from internal coding judgements. This permits a reader to challenge the interpretation of a specific link, for example whether a reference selects work or supplies values, without accepting or rejecting the whole manuscript as a single unit.

## Limits of inference

The analysis used source-location tracking, a frozen codebook, explicit comparison rules, negative cases and a decision trail. The pilot was single-coded and received a second-pass consistency review by the same researcher. The targeted source review was not independent double coding, a live-system test or a study of enacted work. Vendor procedures are selective, include expected results, and may differ in level of detail and version visibility. Absence from a procedure is not evidence that a behaviour or capability is absent from SAP or from customer practice. These limits apply to all findings.
